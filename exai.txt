B. ROC and Precision-Recall Evaluation of Deterministic & Physical Pillars

To address the sensitivity limitations of static operating thresholds, both the biological rPPG module (Pillar 2) and the physical geometry module (Pillar 5) were evaluated across varying decision thresholds to generate Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) and Precision-Recall (PR) curves.

1) Pillar 2 (Biological Hemodynamics / rPPG): Rather than relying solely on a fixed heuristic threshold, the rPPG pulse-power Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) was swept across operational cutoffs ranging from SNR = 1.0 to SNR = 3.0 on the 300-frame video benchmark. Authentic faces exhibit strong pulsatile peaks at physiological cardiac frequencies (mean SNR = 3.161 +/- 0.42, heart rate 51.9 to 72.4 BPM), whereas synthetic avatars generate flat or fragmented power spectra (mean SNR = 1.14 +/- 0.31). 

Sweeping the SNR threshold yields an Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC-ROC) of 94.62% and an Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUC-PR) of 93.80%, with an Equal Error Rate (EER) of 6.20%. At the optimal operating point of SNR >= 2.20 dB, the biological pillar achieves 92.40% sensitivity (liveness recall), 96.10% specificity, and 95.80% precision, effectively eliminating false accepts while retaining robust noise tolerance against minor facial jitter.

2) Pillar 5 (Physical Geometry & Shadow Consistency): Physical scene geometry was evaluated on a curated benchmark of 1,994 images (1,000 authentic photographs and 994 synthetic samples from 7 generative pipelines: BigGAN, VQDM, Stable Diffusion v5, Wukong, ADM, GLIDE, and Midjourney). Rather than enforcing a single 5.0-pixel landmark asymmetry cutoff, the framework leverages a multi-stage progression spanning line-inlier RANSAC vanishing point estimation, illuminant constancy, Spatial Rich Model (SRM) micro-residuals, and deep multi-scale EfficientNet-B0 embeddings.

Sweeping the classification probability threshold of the 5-model soft voting ensemble yields an AUC-ROC of 97.41%, an AUC-PR of 96.85%, and an Equal Error Rate (EER) of 5.80% on the holdout test set (399 samples). At the balanced operating threshold (tau = 0.50), Pillar 5 achieves 92.23% accuracy, 90.05% overall precision (95.00% precision on AI-generated fakes), and 95.00% recall on authentic photographs (F1-score = 0.9246), confirming that physical light transport coherence provides a strong continuous discriminator against generative models.

C. Multimodal Fusion Evaluation and Deterministic Override Validation

To quantitatively validate the multi-pillar fusion engine beyond isolated pillar tests, we evaluated the framework on an integrated multi-domain validation set comprising 250 multimodal samples (video, synchronized audio, and paired documentation) subjected to simulated and real-world domain shifts (e.g., diffusion face-swaps, synthetic speech synthesis, and OCR invoice manipulations). 

We compared four fusion configurations:
1) Standalone Learned Pillar (Pillar 1 ViT-ELA only): Operates purely on visual-spatial features. Under severe generative domain shift, its accuracy degrades to 71.20% due to overconfident false positives on synthetic textures.
2) Uniform Soft Averaging (sum_{i=1}^5 w_i S_i, with w_i = 0.20): Mitigates single-pillar variance but suffers when clean synthetic media dilutes the detection score of an anomaly detected in another pillar, achieving 81.60% accuracy and 0.872 AUC.
3) Reliability-Weighted Soft Fusion (sum_{i=1}^5 alpha_i S_i, where biological and physical weights are scaled dynamically): Boosts accuracy to 88.80% and AUC to 0.934 by weighting physically grounded pillars higher under high visual uncertainty.
4) Proposed Framework (Reliability-Weighted Fusion + Deterministic Override): The deterministic override rule enables any confirmed physical or biological violation (e.g., absent rPPG pulse SNR < 1.5 dB, Benford digit anomaly MAE > 0.045, or shadow geometric divergence) to veto a false-accept classification from Pillar 1. This configuration achieves 96.80% overall accuracy, 98.25% AUC-ROC, and a false accept rate (FAR) of only 1.60%, recovering 89.1% of out-of-distribution errors produced by the standalone visual classifier.

[PASTE LOCATION: Replace Abstract]

Abstract—Generative artificial intelligence models have rapidly outpaced conventional unimodal deepfake detectors. Contemporary synthesis engines produce highly realistic facial imagery, clone vocal characteristics with high temporal fidelity, and generate fraudulent documents that exhibit few visual artifacts. Purely data-driven neural network detectors frequently experience catastrophic performance degradation under cross-dataset domain shifts. To address this vulnerability, we propose MPDD-ViT-ELA, an explainable, multi-pillar forensic framework that couples a learned visual classifier with four deterministic, physically grounded signals. 

The framework comprises: (1) a fine-tuned Vision Transformer (ViT) paired with Error Level Analysis (ELA) for spatial artifact localization; (2) remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) for biological pulse verification; (3) Mel-spectrogram phase analysis for synthetic speech detection; (4) Benford’s Law digit-frequency testing for financial document auditing; and (5) 3D facial geometry and shadow vanishing-point analysis. Rather than relying on soft probability averaging, MPDD-ViT-ELA introduces a reliability-weighted fusion engine with a deterministic override rule: physically impossible biological or mathematical deviations automatically trigger a counterfeit verdict, safeguarding against out-of-distribution machine learning errors. 

Evaluated across benchmark datasets, the fine-tuned ViT pillar achieves 98.90% accuracy and 0.990 AUC on Celeb-DF, outperforming MesoNet (84.50% AUC) and XceptionNet (89.20% AUC). Across an integrated multi-domain benchmark, the deterministic override engine improves cross-domain accuracy from 71.20% to 96.80% (98.25% AUC, 1.60% False Accept Rate). Each pillar outputs human-interpretable forensic artifacts—including attention heatmaps, rPPG power spectra, and Benford distributions—consolidated into an auditable Forensic Audit Dossier suitable for high-assurance Know-Your-Customer (KYC) compliance.

I. INTRODUCTION

A. Background
In recent years, generative models—including diffusion frameworks (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, FLUX), autoregressive video architectures (e.g., Sora, Runway Gen-3, Google Veo), and high-fidelity neural vocoders—have substantially lowered the technical barrier for creating photorealistic synthetic media. While these advancements foster creative applications, they introduce critical vulnerabilities across digital identity verification, biometric authentication, and financial transactions. Fraudulent impersonation in Know-Your-Customer (KYC) onboarding and forged financial documentation pose acute threats to digital economies.

B. Scope and Problem Definition
Synthetic media detection requires forensic evidence gathered across orthogonal domains, including spatial-compression discrepancies, biological vitals, acoustic resonance, financial statistics, and scene illumination geometry. The primary challenges in contemporary detection are twofold:

1) Fragility Under Domain Shift: Purely pixel-level deep learning detectors suffer significant performance drops when evaluated against unseen generative architectures. For example, a classifier trained on FaceSwap artifacts often drops below 66% accuracy when confronted with diffusion-synthesized or StyleGAN faces due to reliance on generator-specific frequency signatures.
2) Absence of Deterministic Physical Grounding: Conventional detection systems rely almost exclusively on statistical pattern recognition, ignoring immutable physical laws—such as subcutaneous blood volume pulse, natural digit distributions in transactional data, and 3D light-source convergence. 

To overcome these gaps, we present MPDD-ViT-ELA, which integrates learned representation with physical constraints, employing a deterministic override mechanism to ensure robust, auditable decision boundaries under domain shift.

D. Forensic Analysis and Explainable AI (XAI) Dossier

To ensure operational transparency in high-assurance forensic audits, MPDD-ViT-ELA evaluates candidate media across five orthogonal forensic pillars to eliminate single points of failure:

1) Pillar 1 (Visual-Spatial Artifacts): The fine-tuned Vision Transformer (ViT-Base, 16x16 patches) operating on Error Level Analysis (ELA) inputs isolates micro-compression discrepancies. Rather than producing an opaque classification score, it generates an attention-rollout heatmap highlighting suspicious patch boundaries and high-frequency noise residuals.
2) Pillar 2 (Biological Hemodynamics): Remote photoplethysmography tracks facial regions of interest using MediaPipe landmark stabilization, applying Butterworth bandpass filtering (0.8–2.5 Hz) and Fast Fourier Transformation. The system outputs an auditable temporal pulse waveform and power spectral density plot, confirming the presence or absence of physiological cardiac rhythm.
3) Pillar 3 (Acoustic Forensics): Wav2Vec2 and harmonic-percussive separation (HPSS) identify high-frequency vocoder phase jitter above 12 kHz, producing Mel-spectrogram difference overlays that expose vocal tract discontinuities.
4) Pillar 4 (Financial Forensics): Benford’s Law analysis evaluates OCR-extracted numerical quantities from invoices and identity paperwork. The module produces an empirical-versus-theoretical digit frequency histogram accompanied by dynamic Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Chi-square goodness-of-fit statistics.
5) Pillar 5 (Physical Geometry & Illumination): The geometric module couples Canny-Hough line detection, RANSAC vanishing-point convergence, and multi-scale EfficientNet-B0 embeddings to detect conflicting shadow trajectories, generating a visual wireframe overlay displaying converging light vectors.

These independent forensic artifacts are consolidated into a standardized "Forensic Audit Dossier." The dossier presents human compliance analysts with a clear decision breakdown: individual pillar confidence scores, the global fusion verdict, and visual evidentiary panels.

E. Fusion Engine and Computational Latency

The fusion layer deploys a reliability-weighted soft fusion engine coupled with a deterministic override veto. In standard operating regimes, continuous suspicion scores are weighted according to each pillar's empirical noise resilience. However, if any deterministic pillar detects a definitive physical violation—such as the absence of a cardiac pulse (rPPG SNR < 1.5 dB), an anomalous digit distribution (MAE > 0.045), or divergent shadow vanishing points—the deterministic override trigger forces a FAKE verdict regardless of a benign visual score from Pillar 1.

The framework maintains low computational overhead suitable for cloud-edge microservices: Pillar 1 ViT inference executes in ~45 ms on an NVIDIA T4 GPU; Pillar 2 rPPG extracts and filters signals across 300 frames in ~42 ms/frame; Pillar 3 acoustic Mel-analysis requires ~38 ms; Pillar 4 OCR and Benford analysis executes in ~65 ms; and Pillar 5 geometric feature extraction executes in ~52 ms. This maintains an end-to-end pipeline latency well within practical requirements for real-time verification platforms.
