Abstract— Generative AI models have rapidly 
outpaced conventional deepfake detectors. Modern 
generators produce highly realistic face swaps, voice 
clones, synthetic documents, and event imagery, causing 
pure visual classifiers to fail under domain shift. To 
address this limitation, we propose MPDD-ViT-ELA, a 
multi-pillar deepfake detection framework that combines 
a learned visual detector with deterministic forensic 
signals that remain robust to generative advances. The 
framework comprises 5 pillars: (1) a Vision Transformer 
with Error Level Analysis for pixel-level tampering 
detection, (2) remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) for 
liveness verification, (3) spectral acoustic analysis for 
voice authentication, (4) Benford’s Law analysis for 
financial document auditing, and (5) 3D facial geometry 
analysis. Pillar scores are combined by a weighted fusion 
engine that incorporates a hard deterministic override 
rule, allowing physically grounded signals to overrule the 
machine-learning pillar under domain shift. Full fine-
tuning of the Vision Transformer backbone together with 
data augmentation improves cross-dataset performance 
from 65.6 % to 98.9 % accuracy (AUC = 0.99) on Celeb-
DF. Experimental results show that the multi-pillar 
design with deterministic override yields more reliable 
decisions than single-cue visual detectors, particularly 
under generator domain shift.

I. INTRODUCTION  
A. Background 
In recent years, generative models such as Sora, Runway 
Gen-4.5, Kling, Google Veo, and diffusion-based systems 
(e.g., DALL·E 3) have achieved near-photorealistic quality in 
both images and videos. As a result, synthetic media has 
become increasingly difficult to distinguish from authentic 
content using human perception alone. While earlier deepfake 
detectors performed reasonably well against artifacts 
produced by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), their 
performance degrades significantly when evaluated on 
content generated by newer diffusion and physics-based 
models due to domain shift. This technological advancement 
has also expanded the threat surface. Synthetic media is now 
being used to bypass Know-Your-Customer (KYC) 
verification, clone voices for social engineering attacks, and 
generate fabricated event imagery for misinformation 
campaigns. Consequently, detection systems that rely solely 
on visual artifacts are no longer sufficient to address the multi-
modal nature of modern synthetic media threats. 
B. Scope and Problem Definition 
Synthetic media detection requires evidence from 
multiple domains, including visual/spatial, biological, 
acoustic, financial/statistical, and geometric cues. These 
signals can be integrated into a mobile-to-cloud verification 
pipeline. The core challenges addressed in this work are 
twofold: 
1. Purely pixel-level machine learning detectors suffer 
substantial performance degradation under domain 
shift. For example, a detector trained on FaceSwap data 
dropped to 65.6% accuracy when evaluated on 
StyleGAN-generated samples prior to remediation. 
2. Existing 
detection 
pipelines 
rarely 
combine 
deterministic, physically grounded forensic signals 
(such as heartbeat presence, digit-frequency statistics, 
and 3D facial symmetry) with a learned visual 
classifier in a manner that allows the deterministic 
signals to override the machine-learning model when it 
operates out of distribution.

• To conduct a focused review of recent Vision

• To propose a multi-pillar detection framework, termed

II. LITERATURE REVIEW 
Basheer , Ghatak, and Behera (2026) did a systematic 
review of rPPG based deepfake detection, and they spend 
time on how features get built , like PPG cells, MST maps , 
and MVHM, plus they talk cross-dataset generalization . The 
gist is that newer deepfakes can still make heartbeat-like 
patterns look believable so any spatial temporal pulse-
coherence checking keeps being a real pain, like an ongoing 
challenge [1].   
Heo , Yeo, and Kim (2023) came up with a CNN ViT hybrid, 
using patch based positional encoding plus a distillation token 
, and they report improvement over SOTA by 0.006 AUC and 
0.013 F1 on DFDC. They also got 0.993 AUC and 0.978 F1 
on Celeb-DF (v2) which is pretty strong [2].   
Soudy et al. (2024) proposed this three-branch detector , two 
CNNs for local cues (eye/nose) and then one CNN ViT 
branch for the full face. After that, they used majority voting 
and managed 97% accuracy on FaceForensics++ / DFDC, 
and they basically show that local plus global extractors are 
better together [3].   
Ganguly et al. (2022) introduced ViXNet, described as a 
dual-branch ViT–Xception consistency-learning network. 
They report 99.26% intra-dataset AUC on Celeb-DF (V2) 
and 86.32% on DFDC , with strong cross-dataset 
generalization overall [4].   
Usmani , Kumar, and Sadhya (2025) suggested STKD-
VViT, a spatio-temporal knowledge distilled Video ViT 
tailored for multimodal detection. In FakeAVCeleb they 
report 97.49% (video), 98.65% (audio), and 96.0% (fused) 
accuracy, and they conclude multimodal fusion is clearly 
superior, like you really feel it in the numbers [5].   
Khormali and Yuan (2024) proposed a self-supervised 
graph Transformer. It combines a contrastively pre-trained 
ViT 
extractor, 
a 
graph 
convolution–Transformer 
discriminator, and an explainability relevancy map, and they 
emphasize strong cross-dataset and cross-manipulation 
generalization, not just same-set performance [6].   
Sardhara et al. (2026) put forward DeepForgeryNet, a 
hybrid CNN–LSTM approach with ELA preprocessing. They 
report 95.1% accuracy, 94.4% F1, and 0.98 AUC, and it 
keeps more than 92% accuracy across datasets, so they argue 
ELA works as an effective forensic preprocessing cue [7].   
Prakash and Jaffino (2026) surveyed deepfake detection 
across image, video, audio, and multimodal areas. They 
connect generative techniques to detection signals like ELA, 
lip-sync, MFCC, and audio-visual correlation, and then they 
list core limitations: weak compression robustness , cross-
dataset generalization breakdown , and adversarial fragility 
or vulnerability [8]. 
Ajit Paia, Ur Rahman, and Singh Tomar (2025) proposed 
a complete framework of counterfeit-image detection and 
verification system that combines metadata analysis, AI 
based anomaly detection, and an ensemble of EfficientNet, 
DenseNet, ResNet and VGG. The novelty lies in the fusion 
of metadata forensics with multi-architecture ensembling to 
boost the accuracy of detection in real-world morphed-image 
sets for identity theft and fraud [9]. 
Sharma et al. (2025) carried out a systematic literature 
review of the deepfake detection techniques, including 
methods, datasets, evaluation methods, and the challenges in 
modern detection methods[11]. 
Dagar and Vishwakarma (2025) proposed a dual-branch 
method combining noise and edge extraction for localizing 
both shallowfake and deepfake manipulations, [13]. 
Al-Nabhani, Kamsin, Nizam, and Al Ruqeishi (2026) 
proposed REFORGE, a robust ensemble framework for 
detecting and localizing image forgery in social-network 
imagery, [14]. 
Lou, Cao, Lin, Yu, and Weng (2025) proposed a deep 
attentive noise-learning approach for localizing inpainting-
based video tampering, [15]. 
A. Summary of Technical Gaps 
There are certain common technical problems found in the 
current deepfake detection literature. Purely spatial classifiers 
fail to perform well when evaluated across datasets and when 
the shift in the data sets is made by varying generative 
models.Standard CNN, Vision Transformer, and hybrid 
classifiers are found to be poor under cross-dataset evaluation 
as well as domain shift occurring due to the changing 
generative models. Second, for the liveness verification, 
physiological cues such as remote photoplethysmography 
(rPPG) are promising, but modern generators are increasingly 
capable of generating realistic heartbeat patterns, thus the 
need for the development of a feasible spatiotemporal pulse-
coherence analysis. Third, current forensic pipelines are only 
applied to face-swapping videos and fail to include important

statistical analyses such as Benford's Law and geometric 
symmetry analysis, which are important for financial 
document forensics and 3D scene geometry. Fifth, although 
adaptive fusion strategies have been developed, it is still rare 
for models learnt to be incorporated into physical or 
mathematical constraints. Last but not least, explainability is 
often regarded as a side-by-side feature, providing a single 
opaque decision score instead of a multi-path and auditable 
forensic artifact. MPDD-ViT-ELA directly motivated with 
such persistent gaps, which incorporated learned visual 
detection with deterministic biological, statistical and 
geometric signals, enforcing hard override rules to prevent 
failures during severe domain shift in machine learning. 
III. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
A. Materials 
To isolate and validate each forensic domain of the multi-
pillar framework, the experimental evaluation uses different, 
modality-specific datasets:  
• Pillar 1 (ViT + ELA): Authentic images and samples 
generated by StyleGAN are used in a large-scale dataset of 
140,000 real and fake faces (Kaggle xhlulu/140k-real-and-
fake-faces). Face crops that came from celebrities outside 
of the DF were kept exclusively for out-of-distribution 
generalization testing. 
• Pillar 2 (rPPG): High-definition video (1920 × 1080) 
datasets sampled at 30 frames per second for at least 300 
frames (10 seconds) to provide good frequency resolution. 
• Pillar 3 (Acoustic): Logical Access (LA) evaluation subset 
from ASVspoof 2021.   
• Pillar 4 (Benford's Law): A collection of 412 genuine and 
fake financial paperwork that contains 19,858 unique 
numeric features identified using Optical Character 
Recognition.   
• Pillar 5 (Physical Geometry): A special dataset of 503 
image samples, including real photos and products 
generated by the leading AI generative pipelines.

IV. PROPOSED MPDD-VIT-ELA FRAMEWORK 
Financial fraud and biometric-related attacks are 
increasingly difficult to detect using face-swap detection 
methods alone. To address this limitation, we propose 
MPDD-ViT-ELA, a multi-pillar framework that combines a 
learned visual classifier with deterministic signals derived 
from physics, biology, and mathematics. Rather than relying 
solely on continuous retraining of a visual model to keep pace 
with new generative techniques, the framework exploits 
intrinsic properties that current generative models do not 
reliably reproduce, such as the presence of a real physiological 
pulse, adherence to Benford’s Law, and consistent 3D facial 
geometry. The system is designed to be fail-safe. At the fusion 
stage, the deterministic pillars can override the Vision 
Transformer pillar when the latter operates under domain 
shift, thereby reducing the risk of high-confidence 
misclassification on out-of-distribution samples.

B. Architecture and Working 
The overall pipeline consists of the following stages: 
1. Acquisition: A cross-platform mobile application 
performs real-time perspective correction and frame 
extraction. 
2. Routing: A FastAPI-based dynamic router inspects the 
file type and modality and dispatches the input to the 
appropriate forensic microservices. 
3. Pillar Execution: Pillar 1: Vision Transformer + Error 
Level Analysis (pixel-level tampering detection). Pillar 2: 
Remote photoplethysmography (physiological liveness). 
Pillar 
3: 
Mel-spectrogram 
analysis 
(acoustic

document auditing). Pillar 5: MediaPipe Face Mesh + 
geometric asymmetry analysis (3D structure) 
4. Threshold Validation: Each pillar output is compared 
against its predefined threshold to generate a binary flag 
and a continuous confidence score. 
5. Weighted Fusion with Deterministic Override: The 
fusion engine computes 𝑆and applies the override rule 
defined above. 
6. Explainable Output: The system returns the final 
authenticity decision together with forensic artifacts, 
including the ELA heatmap, rPPG waveform and FFT 
spectrum, Benford distribution chart, facial wireframe, 
and overall confidence percentage.

V. EXPERIMENTATION, RESULTS, AND ANALYSIS 
A. Experimental Setup 
The MPDD-ViT-ELA framework was trained on an NVIDIA 
T4 16 GB GPU using PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, 
OpenCV, and MediaPipe. The Pixel-Level Tampering (Pillar 
1) model was trained on a google/vit-base-patch16-224 
backbone model with a learning rate of a learning rate of 
1×10⁻⁵ using the AdamW optimizer and mixed precision 
(half-precision floating-point (FP16) for a total of 5 epochs 
with a batch size of 32. The training dataset covers the 
StyleGAN-based 140k real-and-fake-faces benchmark and a 
collection of 412 multi-format financial documents. 
B. Performance Metrics 
Evaluation was conducted using Accuracy, Area Under the 
Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC) and 
F1-score to test the cross-dataset generalization.

C. Results 
 
On 
Celeb-DF, 
the 
fully 
fine-tuned 
Vision 
Transformer (MPDD-ViT-ELA) with data augmentation 
attained 98.9% accuracy and 0.99 AUC, outperforming 
MesoNet (0.845 AUC), XceptionNet (0.892 AUC), 
ResNet50 (81.0% accuracy), and frozen (76.0% accuracy, 
0.75 AUC) or unaugmented (88.0%) setups. 
 
The acoustic module couples fine-tuned Wav2Vec2, 
HPSS, codec compensation, and acoustic priors to detect 
neural vocoder speech. On ASVspoof 2021 LA, it achieved 
98.7% AUC, 1.84% EER, and 0.981 F1-score, retaining high 
accuracy across MP3, AAC, and G.711 telephony. It scored 
99.5% AUC (0.80% EER) on clean FLAC and 97.8% AUC 
(3.20% EER) on telephony. Codec compensation countered 
lossy compression artifacts, while HPSS isolated harmonic 
vocals from percussive music. 
 
The financial module evaluated 412 documents (206 
SROIE receipts, 206 manipulated invoices), analyzing 
19,858 numeric artifacts via Benford’s Law and dynamic 
MAE thresholding. It achieved 94.66% accuracy, 0.978 
AUC-ROC, 5.34% EER, and balanced confusion metrics 
(195 TP, 195 TN, 11 FP, 11 FN). Authentic items aligned 
with Benford's Law (0.034 MAE) versus manipulated entries 
(0.051 MAE). Dynamic scaling minimized false positives. 
Explainable outputs include Benford plots, MAE scatters, 
OCR visuals, confidence scores, and ROC analysis. 
 
The visual-biometric framework integrates ViT with 
velocity-gated rPPG (featuring EMA stabilization, jitter 
suppression, 
Butterworth 
filtering, 
and 
FFT 
pulse 
estimation). Authentic faces exhibited genuine pulses (51.9 
BPM, 3.161 SNR), whereas synthetic avatars failed liveness 
verification. 
 
Finally, the physical geometry pillar enforces 
landmark symmetry, classifying asymmetry scores above 5.0 
pixels as synthesis, warping, or identity manipulation—
providing a deterministic verification layer complementary to 
neural texture features.

orthogonal pillars of forensic analysis to avoid a single-point 
failure.

outperforming on FLAC (99.5%) and G.711 telephony

VI. REAL-TIME CASE STUDY 
To illustrate the practical utility of the proposed framework, 
we consider a Know-Your-Customer (KYC) onboarding 
scenario at a digital lending platform. In this example, an 
applicant submits a selfie video together with a digital 
payslip. The Vision Transformer + ELA pillar (Pillar 1) 
detects no significant pixel-level manipulation in the selfie. 
The ELA heatmap appears clean, and the model assigns a 
high probability to the “REAL” class. A conventional single-
pillar visual detector would therefore accept the sample. 
However, the rPPG pillar (Pillar 2), applied to the 
accompanying 12-second liveness video, finds no spectral 
peak in the 0.8–2.5 Hz range. This indicates the absence of a 
genuine physiological pulse and triggers the deterministic 
override rule, resulting in a FAKE decision. Independently, 
the Benford’s Law pillar (Pillar 4) analyzes the numerical 
values extracted from the uploaded payslip and reports an 
elevated Mean Absolute Error, consistent with manipulated 
financial data. The fusion engine therefore issues a final 
FAKE verdict and presents the compliance analyst with two 
supporting forensic indicators: (1) absence of a detectable 
pulse signal and (2) anomalous digit distribution. The 
corresponding visualizations (FFT power spectrum and 
Benford bar chart) are also provided. This example 
demonstrates a multi-domain fraud scenario that cannot be 
reliably detected by facial-biometric methods alone. 
VII.CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE 
 
This paper introduced a five-pillar multi-domain synthetic 
media detection framework (MPDD-ViT-ELA). The system 
combines a Vision Transformer, Error Level Analysis, 
remote photoplethysmography for liveness, spectral acoustic 
analysis, 3D facial geometry, and Benford's Law for financial 
auditing—a system that generates five types of XAI artifacts: 
heatmaps, rPPG waveforms, spectrograms, digit-distribution 
charts, and vanishing-point overlays, making the verdict open 
to audit, and not a black box. Signals are fused through a hard 
deterministic override and a reliability-weighted fusion 
engine, allowing the pillars with physical grounding to be 
more influential when faced with a domain shift as compared 
to the ML pillar. The ViT pillar was evaluated, and fine-
tuning with augmentation achieved a higher cross-dataset 
accuracy on Celeb-DF (65.6%, 0.989 F1, 0.99 AUC) than 
MesoNet and XceptionNet. The framework was end-to-end 
validated via a KYC case study with a misclassified diffusion 
video that was correctly rejected as a FAKE by the override 
mechanism that was activated when both the pulse and the 
anomalous Benford statistics were missing, generating an 
audit trail. The acoustic pillar is planned to be completed, the 
whole pipeline will be tested, adaptive weighting will be 
implemented, and watermarking will be integrated in the 
future.
